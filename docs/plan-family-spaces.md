# Plan: Family Spaces (v5)

Status: In review (v4 through two external reviews; a third fresh-session review folded)
Date: 2026-10-05
Author: Esteban
Related: docs/plan-sharing.md (closed design record), docs/plan-merged-search.md (G24), D59-D78 in docs/software-architecture-decisions.md

## 1. Problem and ruling

A family Space is not a small organization, but the product owner has ruled that scoping is a requirement: family members must be able to see only what is shared with them, and some items must stay hidden from some members. Earlier revisions (v2/v3) modeled the family as an unflipped vault where every member decrypts everything, with privacy living only in personal vaults and separate Spaces. That model is dropped. v3's umbrella of several family vaults is also dropped: there is exactly one family vault, `baluard-family`.

The v4 model is a single vault, born in team shape, with roles and collections as the access machinery. This is deliberately Bitwarden-shaped, and Baluard already ships every primitive it needs: roles, collection grants to members and groups, scoped key wraps, kit recovery, quorum asks. What gets designed here is the family ergonomics on top, so it never feels like an org console. The Space slug prefix applies: the vault normalizes to `baluard-space-family`.

## 2. Model: team-shaped from birth

A family vault is created directly in team shape (the shape organizations use). Today nothing creates a team-shaped vault directly and `space_flip` does not refuse a personal vault (its only gates are unlocked, the manage gate, and member readiness, so a personal founder would pass), so the creation path is defined as create-then-flip inside one wizard transaction, with a new refusal: `space_flip` refuses every vault whose purpose is not `family` or `org` (personal vaults refuse, and pair Spaces refuse too: they carry the Space flag and would otherwise flip and break their two-person semantics). One implemented refinement: the shipped org-flip surfaces call `space.flip` directly, so a purpose-less personal vault flipping stamps `purpose: org` at the flip surface itself (org conversion is what a personal flip is); a legacy pair Space still refuses because its Space flag marks it. Consequences, all existing behavior:

- Admin devices hold the Space Group Key and see every item, filed or not.
- Member devices (Editor/Viewer) hold neither the Root Key nor the SGK. They receive wrapped collection keys only for collections granted to them, so an ungranted collection is not filtered out of their view; it is cryptographically absent.
- The sync policy admits cipher writes for a member holding Editor rank plus a write grant on the item's collection, and refuses Viewer-rank writes regardless of grants (the role floor is pinned by test). A child who should edit joins as Editor, and the grants alone bound their reach.
- Collections carry `Read`, `Write`, and `manage` access with expiry dates, grantable to members or groups.

The `purpose` flag is an enum with four states: absent, `pair`, `family`, `org`. Absent plus the Space flag means a legacy pair Space and keeps its two-person cap, so no existing vault changes meaning. Absent plus a flipped shape means a legacy org and stays one. Absent plus neither means a personal vault. The flag lives in plaintext `meta/settings-pub.json` beside the shape marker, written by an Owner-only route (no quorum claim: the file carries no approval envelope; structural delta validation is future work), and a change to it posts a notice to every member device so a co-guardian cannot silently re-label the vault. `purpose: family` drives defaults: `personal_merge` on, minors available, family kit rules, join notices. `purpose: org` keeps the organization behavior unchanged.

`purpose: family` is writable only at creation, on an empty vault, inside the wizard; a populated vault can never gain it, which is the enforcement behind "a personal vault never converts into a family vault". `purpose: org` is writable from a personal vault, because that is how organizations are made today: create a plain vault, flip it to team shape. Classification follows the union, not one signal: a vault is a Space when it carries a purpose, a flipped shape, or the Space flag, which is also what G24 and the D31 desktop merge must check, since org vaults made through the flip path carry neither the flag nor a purpose and would otherwise classify as personal and always be merge-eligible.

One adoption cost is stated rather than hidden: v1 has no in-app path to found a family vault from an existing populated personal vault; the founder rides plaintext export and import, and the selective "copy these items to my new family vault" flow is v1.1. The wizard makes every step idempotent and resumable (the flip is irreversible, so a crash after it leaves a resumable team-shaped vault, not a broken one), and a failed Space-flag write inside the wizard is a hard failure, not the warn-and-continue `space.create` uses today, because the flag now gates quorum and merge.

The flag split is load-bearing. Today `space.create` writes `"space": true`, and that flag caps the vault at two members, refuses Admin and Owner invites, and makes the ownership swap demote the old Owner to Editor, which would break the divorce journey. v4 therefore makes both consumers purpose-aware: `pair_gate` caps only `purpose: pair` (the couple stays a couple), and on `purpose: family` the swap keeps the old Owner at Admin so a departing guardian can be removed after the claim. The family vault is a Space (`space: true`) with `purpose: family`, which is also what makes G24 classify it correctly; without the split, a family created through the plain vault path would be treated as a personal vault by the merge ceiling, and a family created through the Space path could never seat the second guardian. Quorum stays at 1: `space_settings_set` refuses `quorum_admin_ops` above 1 on a family vault until the v1.1 contest window ships, so the last-guardian deadlock cannot be configured.

## 3. Roles and scopes

Roles stay `Owner/Admin/Editor/Viewer` and gate administration and rank exactly as organizations use them. Collections are the content boundary:

- `Shared`: created automatically with the vault and granted Read to every member at join. Wi-Fi, streaming, shared cards. This is what keeps the family vault from becoming a grants console: the common items need no thought.
- Group-restricted collections: one group (named whatever the household calls it, `Guardians` in this plan) holds the collections some members must never see (`Finances`, `Documents`). Granting a collection to the group is the whole act; membership in the group is the visibility rule. The group bounds non-admin members; it is not a boundary between admins, who hold the SGK and unwrap every collection key regardless of group membership (section 9 states the same plainly). The group earns its keep for trusted adults without admin rank, an aunt or a grandparent who should read `Shared` but never `Finances`.
- Per-person collections: each child gets their own (`Kids-Alex`, `Kids-Peter`, `Kids-Sam`), granted Write to that child and Read to the guardians (admins see everything regardless). One shared `Kids` collection would let a twelve-year-old read and edit a seven-year-old's entries.
- Adding an item defaults to `Shared` **by cipher type**, and the default binds Admins only: Login and SecureNote go to `Shared`; Card, Identity, BankAccount, DriversLicense, and Passport go admin-only (unfiled) or to the `Guardians` collection. Members cannot write unfiled ciphers on a team vault, so a child's own additions default to their granted writable collection; the alternative would fail closed with a permission error on every child-saved card. The reason for the admin-side split is that `Shared` is a leak by omission if a guardian dumps the family card in it, and because moving an item out of `Shared` later is not a recall: member clones already hold the earlier ciphertext in git history and the collection key never changed. The UI states this plainly where items are moved out of shared collections.
- `Shared` is a protected system collection: its id is recorded in admin-written settings, deleting it is refused, and deleting any non-empty collection is refused (today `collection_delete` orphans its items and the policy then denies all writes into the unknown directory).
- Role-gated collection defaults ("visible to Admins and above automatically") are v1.1 sugar over groups; groups carry v1 completely.

Two boundaries are stated rather than hidden. First, scoping is confidentiality-only: an Editor child's writes to `entries/**` (attachments), `meta/folders/**`, and `meta/tags/**` are not grant-scoped (collection trash is grant-scoped, and flat trash is admin-only), so a child could damage attachments on a hidden item or rename family folders and tags; git history makes this recoverable, and the creation flow says so. Second, some metadata stays member-visible: which collections exist, their grant lists, item counts and size tiers (collection directory names are salted, but the definitions under `meta/collections/` expose names and grants), plus folders, tags, profile, settings, and contacts (re-sealed under the member key at flip). Guardians are advised not to encode secrets in collection, folder, or tag names.

Self-escalation is impossible: grants are minted by Admins, `manage` delegation keeps grantees from editing the lists that delegate to them, and collection-creation authority follows `collection_create_authority`, which already defaults to admins.

## 4. The concrete family

One household, one vault. The composition is arbitrary: any number of adults at any rank, any number of minors, and the admins are the guardians regardless of legal relationship. The example below shows the spread:

| Member | Rank | Grants |
|---|---|---|
| Mom (Owner) | Owner | everything, admin surface |
| Dad (Admin) | Admin | everything, admin surface |
| Grandma (Admin) | Admin | everything, co-guardian, lives with the family |
| Uncle Rob (Viewer) | Viewer | Read `Shared` only |
| Alex (12, Editor) | Editor | Read `Shared`, Read+Write `Kids-Alex` |
| Peter (10, Editor) | Editor | Read `Shared`, Read+Write `Kids-Peter` |
| Sam (7, Editor) | Editor | Read `Shared`, Read+Write `Kids-Sam` |

Peter is a cousin's child living with the family without formal adoption: a flagged minor whose guardians are the household admins, because the vault records no legal relationship and needs none. Grandma as a third admin keeps every kit operation satisfiable through any single loss. Uncle Rob reads the common items and nothing else; granting him `Finances` later is one grant.

Alex opens search: personal vault, `Shared`, `Kids-Alex`, merged and badged (G24). `Finances` does not appear as locked or empty; Alex's device holds no key for it. Alex saves the new game account into `Kids-Alex` and edits it freely. Nobody ever configures a role matrix.

## 5. Membership states: the minor flag

Membership gains a `minor` state. It is guardian-controlled, never an age claim: no birthdates, no verification, no legal status recorded, ever. A guardian is simply an Admin of the vault, so a ward taken in without adoption works identically to a biological child: flagged, restricted, guarded by the household's admins. Because member rows are plaintext on the forge, the flag does not join them; it lives in an admin-written sealed record under the member's own subtree, so nothing about the child's status leaks to the remote, the sync delta validator needs no new pinned field, and no `MemberFile` construction site changes. One policy row rides along: the members table admits any role to write under its own subtree (that is what carries leave records), so an explicit Admin-only row for the flag record lands ahead of it, or a minor could overwrite or delete the sealed flag from a validly signed commit. An Admin-only route `members.set_minor` flips it.

A minor cannot be promoted, cannot claim ownership, cannot use the kit, cannot approve asks, and cannot be a swap or claim target: the ownership swap and the claim route gain a target guard that requires a live, non-minor row everywhere and Admin rank on family purpose only, because today the claim accepts any live row and the swap checks only tombstones; pair and org vaults keep their existing transfer semantics, and a pair Space has no Admin rank at all. A minor files a leave request, but nothing executes without an Admin approving it (section 6). Growing up is a flag flip with no crypto event; there is deliberately no role-change route, so promoting a former minor to Admin runs the standard remove-and-re-invite path (fresh member id, grants re-minted), and the wizard states it. Children join as Editors by default on family purpose, with their grants bounding what the rank can touch; Viewer remains available for read-only members, and the policy floor guarantees a Viewer cannot write even where granted. The orphan escape carries from v3: possession of the kit phrase clears the minor flag. If every guardian is gone, the adult child recovers with the safe-deposit phrase and the flag falls with it.

## 6. Departure: an approved leave record

The v3 design carries unchanged: the departing member writes a signed leave record into their own member subtree, every admin surface shows a pending-departure notification, an Admin explicitly approves, and that single execution runs the standard ceremony (seat strike, SGK rotation, tombstone with a coarse `left` versus `removed` reason). The leaver sees "waiting for a guardian to finish". Minors file the same record; the Admin approval is the guardian's say. `members.leave` refuses the Owner row until ownership is transferred. Org purpose keeps admin-only removal.

One property improves under team shape: a member's export contains only what they can decrypt, so a leaver walking away with an export takes their granted scope, not the vault. The export gate itself blocks Viewer rank only, so an Editor child can always export their own granted scope; that is acceptable on family purpose because their scope is their things plus `Shared`, and the export warning names it. Per-item handovers ride guest reveals, which carry Login credentials only today; notes, cards, and identities move by direct sharing or an admin-run export. The selective "copy these items to my vault" flow remains v1.1.

The cryptographic cut for a departing member is not the SGK rotation. Rotation re-seals the same collection keys (the CK value never changes: a recorded S3-7c boundary), and an Admin leaver who unwrapped every collection key through the SGK still holds working keys after any Root/SGK rotation. v1 therefore ships a `collection_rotate_key` operation, built on the expiry sweep's existing machinery (collect, fresh key, survivors re-wrapped, entries re-encrypted), and the approval execution runs it for every collection the leaver could open: all of them when the leaver is an Admin. The operation also prunes the leaver's rows from the collection definitions; the sweep prunes only expired grants, and rewrapping alone would leave dead grant records behind. The same execution re-issues the kit with a new phrase before pushing, because the old phrase unwraps the freshly rotated Root Key until re-issuance. When the leaver owns the repo, the execution requires `space.migrate` so transport does not die with their account.

Grant narrowing before an approval is advisory, not cryptographic: revoking a grant removes the row and the wrap but does not rotate the collection key (a recorded D67 boundary), and the old wrap file remains in git history, so a still-seated member could unwrap the unchanged key. The approval execution above is the cryptographic event; narrowing first is still good hygiene.

## 7. Kits and the repo

Phrase-only family recovery carries from v3: the current kit recovery demands the vault master password and binds to the first live Owner row, which fails the grandparent-with-phrase-only journey. Family purpose redesigns recovery: the 24 words unwrap the Root Key (they are the root authority by D64), the recovering device mints its own fresh master password and keystore, and it always ends as a fresh member row: Owner when no live admin row exists, otherwise Admin alongside the survivor. Recovery clears the minor flag.

Publishing is the hard part, and v4 missed it. A recovered device is a fresh identity, so every peer resolves its first commit against the previous roster, which lacks it, and refuses; the D78 enrollment fallback never fires on a flipped vault, recovery writes no enrollment proof, and members hold no Root Key to verify one anyway. The fix rides the kit file: at kit creation the admin writes into it the public half of an Ed25519 key derived from the phrase. The recovered device signs its first commit with that phrase-derived key; peers accept that commit under it, adopt the updated roster from its tree, and verify everything after under the device key. The check rides both the pull walk and the clone walk, so a vault cloned after a recovery verifies the same history the same way. The recovering device must also be able to clone, which requires repo read access, so the kit checklist names the seat prerequisite: the phrase plus repo access recovers the vault; the phrase alone recovers it once someone with access invites the recovered device in.

Kit rules carry: enabling over an existing kit or disabling one requires confirmation from one other admin, a threshold of two bounded by the live-admin count so a sole admin (the founding state, or after losses) confirms alone, counted over currently-live non-tombstoned rows. The three-admin household keeps kit operations possible through any single loss. Any guardian's approved departure or the succession journey forces a kit re-issue with a new phrase.

The repo outlives people, as before: seats and strikes act with the acting device's token, so when the repo-owning guardian departs or dies a surviving admin runs `space.migrate` to move the vault to their own account. Both checklists name it. One boundary rides along: peers learn the new address from the announcement pushed to the old remote, so if the old repo vanishes before members pull, each member re-adds the vault by the new address manually. Child devices need a forge seat to sync, and a child cannot hold one (most forges require an adult account), so v1 gives child devices a guardian-owned read-write seat scoped to the family repo, clearly labeled in the wizard as the guardian's forge identity, or leaves the child local-only until they can hold their own seat. A write seat means a child could force-push the family repo; history makes that recoverable provided an admin clone exists, and the creation flow says to keep one.

## 8. Lifecycle matrix

Arrival (a new spouse, a grandparent moving in, an aunt, a cousin's child taken in without formal adoption): the invite flow, join queue, and `join_approval: manual|code` unchanged. New members receive Read on `Shared` at join plus whatever else the inviting guardian grants. Minors join flagged.

Growing up: a guardian flips `minor` off via `members.set_minor`. No crypto event; grants unchanged.

Founding their own family: create a new family vault, copy out what is yours (section 6), file the leave record.

Separation or divorce: no automatic split. The leaving guardian transfers ownership first if they hold it (the purpose-aware swap keeps them Admin rather than demoting to Editor), files the leave record, another guardian approves; the approval execution rotates the collection keys the leaver could open, re-issues the kit, migrates the repo if the leaver owned it, and the notice says "you left". What a member could decrypt, they could always have copied; scoping bounds what was ever visible, not what memory retains. Grant narrowing before approval is advisory (section 6). Co-parenting after separation is the guardians' arrangement; collections and grants already express it.

Death of a guardian: a surviving admin claims ownership, removes the deceased row, re-issues the kit, migrates the repo if needed, all at quorum 1. A household whose last admin dies: the phrase-only kit recovery is the path, and family creation makes the kit mandatory. A relative recovering binds to a live Admin row or takes the Owner seat, then may claim. Every guardian gone: the kit phrase lives wherever the family's most important paper lives.

Stolen device: a reset alone is not a theft response, and on a team vault neither is a root rotation alone. The guardian does four things. `member_reset` kills the identity (it tombstones the row, prunes groups, and drops the space wrap, but performs no rotation and strikes no provider token). The stolen device is revoked, striking its seat and provider token. The collections the device could open get an explicit collection-key rotation via the new `collection_rotate_key` operation (section 6), because re-keying alone re-encrypts under the same collection key a thief may already have extracted. The replacement invite carries the Shared template plus re-granted per-child collections, because a reset successor starts with no grants and no group memberships and the child would otherwise lose everything until a guardian notices. A stolen Admin device is simpler: revoke-and-rekey rotates the Root Key and SGK, and the collection rotation still applies to collections only admins could open.

An invited co-guardian who never joins: admin-count and kit-confirm assumptions rise only when they actually join; unclaimed invites expire.

## 9. Non-goals

No death detection, inactivity triggers, or automatic succession; co-admins plus kit are the answers. No automatic asset division. No age verification. No legal-relationship modeling: guardianship is whoever holds admin rank, so wards, step-families, and multi-generation households need no special cases. No new cryptographic primitives: scoping is the shipped team shape. The org model stays untouched for organizations. Hiding is real (absent keys) for content, never for metadata (section 3), and stated plainly in the creation flow: admins always see everything, so a child's privacy from the guardians is not a thing this vault provides; that is what personal vaults are. The last admin dying leaves the members able to read their granted collections but nobody able to grant or rotate; the kit is the designed path out, and creation makes it mandatory.

## 10. Sync and enrollment safety

The D78 enrollment-proof fallback never fires on a flipped vault at all, regardless of any verifier: the pull path skips it outright because a structural pass would let any remote writer forge admissions, and kit-recovered devices publish through the phrase-derived verifier key of section 7 instead. Members cannot self-grant: grant lists are admin-written, `manage` delegation cannot edit the lists that delegate, and the sync policy verifies collection-scoped writes against rank plus grant on every pull. One quorum-1 sharp edge is accepted and surfaced: at threshold 1 an Admin guardian can claim ownership from a live Owner unilaterally, so v1 ships a notice to the Owner's devices when a claim against a live Owner executes (at quorum 1 there is no pending ask, so the notice rides the claim's commit), and the claim target guard of section 5 stops claims against minors or non-Admin rows (the D61 contest window that makes claims contestable remains v1.1).

## 11. G24 wiring

`purpose: family` sets the `personal_merge` default on, and the family vault classifies as a Space under the union rule of section 2. The org half of that rule matters as much: org vaults created through the plain-vault-plus-flip path carry neither a purpose nor the Space flag today, so the merge ceiling must classify on flipped shape too or every organization would merge silently. The merged set is bound to what the member can decrypt (the D71 scoped-key rule), so a child's merged search and autofill span their personal vault, `Shared`, and their granted collections, badged; nothing surfaces from `Finances` because nothing decrypts. Guardians merging the family vault see their full view; a grandparent with `Shared`-only grants merges exactly that. Slug-scoped fetches, service-worker projection, and the ranking rules in docs/plan-merged-search.md apply unchanged; family purpose only pre-seeds the consent default.

## 13. Landing order

1. Flags, classification, and shape: `purpose` (enum with legacy semantics) + the sealed minor record with `members.set_minor` and its Admin-only policy row placed ahead of the members subtree row; the pair-gate split (including legacy `space:true`) and the purpose-aware swap with the family-scoped target guard; the `space_flip` refusal and the create-then-flip wizard transaction (idempotent, resumable, hard-fail on the flag write); `Shared` created at vault creation and protected as a system collection; non-empty collection deletion refused.
2. Crypto operations the journeys depend on: `collection_rotate_key` built on the sweep machinery, including pruning the removed member's grant rows; the kit file gains the phrase-derived verifier key and both the pull and clone walks accept a recovered device's first commit under it.
3. Shared ergonomics: join-time `Shared` Read via the invite role-template grants; add-cipher defaults routing by cipher type. LANDED (2026-10-06): family invites without an explicit template embed Shared Read inline; unfiled admin logins/notes default to Shared, sensitive types stay flat, and member additions route to their single Write-granted collection (zero or several refuse with plain-language errors).
4. `members.leave` record route, pending-departure surface, admin approval execution (CK rotation, kit re-issue, migrate), reason-based notices; the grant-narrowing-is-advisory wording in the UI. LANDED (2026-10-06): members.leave / leave_list / leave_approve routes; the approval captures the leaver's openable collections first (ALL of them for an Admin, whose SGK unwraps every key; expired grants included), strikes, tombstones with reason `left`, rotates those collections pruning the leaver's rows, re-issues the kit with the new phrase returned exactly once, refuses while the leaver owns the repo (case-insensitive), and surfaces rotation or re-issue failures in the response instead of swallowing them. 5. Kit work: phrase-only family recovery ending in a fresh Owner-or-Admin row, fixed-threshold confirmations, departure-forced re-issue, the seat prerequisite in the checklist.
